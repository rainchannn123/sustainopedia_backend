However, only a few systematic analyses on the environmental burdens and improvements of China's crude steel production have been conducted. Therefore, it is important for research to be done how China's steel industry can be improved in environment management. To help decision-makers understand this, a life cycle water footprint analysis in- cluding gray and blue water was performed based on the methodology prescribed in the ISO 14046 standard. A life cycle assessment was also conducted to improve the environmental performance of the steel industry. Results of these assessments revealed that gray water footprint, which is mainly derived from aquatic eutrophication, carcinogens, and non-carcinogens, is higher than blue water footprint. Optimizing indirect processes, including iron ore mining, magnesium oxide production, transportation, and electricity generation, played dominant roles in the reduction of gray water footprint. Furthermore, COD, Cr (VI), phosphate, BOD 5 , Hg, As, nitrogen oxides, particulates, and sulfur dioxide were the key substances for environmental improvements. The underestimation of direct water footprint showed the importance and urgency of implementing scienti ﬁ c and adequate mon- itoring indicators. Meanwhile, the environmental burden can be reduced by adopting a reasonable location of the steel industry on the basis of regional water resources and actual transportation status, improving the ef- ﬁ ciency of raw material consumption, and optimizing the power structure. 1. Introduction Water resource shortage is a common global problem, and the world is predicted to face about 40% global water de ﬁ cit in 2030 ( UNESCO, 2015 ). The China is also identi ﬁ ed with water scarcity ( UNESCO, 2015 ), and approximately 32% of the water in China faces various pollution problems ( NBSC, 2016 ). China's iron and steel industry (ISI), which has been the world's largest crude steel production industry since 1996 ( WSA, 2016 ), su ﬀ ers from serious water resources shortage and environmental pollution problems ( Gu et al., 2015 ;  MPI, 2016 ). For example, 14.5% of the total wastewater and gas is produced from ISI in China ( Wu et al., 2017 ). Moreover, the freshwater consumed by China's ISI accounts for approximately 14% of the total industrial water con- sumption of China ( Huang et al., 2011 ). The wastewater discharged from ISI contains numerous toxic pollutants (e.g., heavy metals, waste oil, and cyanide) that lead to signi ﬁ cant environmental degeneration ( Gu et al., 2015 ). Meanwhile, ISI is highly energy intensive which caused inseparable association with water resource shortage and var- ious pollutants emissions during its entire life cycle ( Olmez et al., 2016 ). Those environmental impacts can be passed through the supply chain to downstream products and signi ﬁ cantly in ﬂ uence the global environment ( Berger et al., 2012 ;  Wang et al., 2014 ). To deal with water resource problems caused by ISI, the Chinese government has enacted increasingly stringent standards to control wastewater from ISI and encourage enterprises to improve water consumption and recycling e ﬃ ciency ( MPI, 2016 ). A systematic assessment of water discharge and consumption is therefore necessary. Water footprint (WF) is a comprehensive indicator for assessing water resource consumption and pollution caused by anthropogenic activities in geographical and temporal dimensions ( Hoekstra et al., 2011 ). This indicator considers direct and indirect processes. To the best of our knowledge, only few analytical studies on WF have ex- amined crude steel production. One of these limited studies is that of  Gu et al. (2015) , who performed a case study on ISI in Eastern China via chain summation and stepwise accumulative approach. However, this study hardly identi ﬁ ed the key factors that contribute to WF during the whole life cycle. The In- ternational Organization for Standardization (ISO) proposed the life cycle assessment (LCA) method as an international standard for WF analyses to address these problems ( ISO 14046, 2014 ). LCA is a pow- erful method that quanti ﬁ es various environmental e ﬀ ects associated with the entire life cycle of certain products, processes, or activities ( Finnveden et al., 2009 ). Currently, most life cycle WF analysis is or- iented toward the impact of water scarcity on human health and eco- system ( Hoekstra, 2016 ;  Berger et al., 2014 ;  Boulay et al., 2011 ). However, the toxicity impact which is related to the changes in water quality also plays indispensable roles in WF evaluation ( ISO 14046, 2014 ;  Ridoutt and P ﬁ ster, 2013 ). The limited research based on toxicity impact uses the LCA model (e.g., ReCiPe model) directly ( Ridoutt and P ﬁ ster, 2013 ). However, direct application of the LCA model to WF analyses produces inaccurate result ( Ma et al., 2018 ;  Bulle et al., 2013 ). ISO also pointed out that only the in ﬂ uence related to water quality should be considered in WF analysis ( ISO 14046, 2014 ). Meanwhile, few studies have focused on the LCA analysis of crude steel production. These LCA studies are meaningful for other environment issues, such as global warming and fossil depletion.  Rossi et al. (2017)  focused on the life cycle assessment of steel bridge.  Bieda (2012)  reported the life cycle inventory of Polish steel production, and  Burchart-Korol (2013)  and Olmez et al. (2016)  conducted LCA analysis of Polish and Turkish ISI, respectively. However, the LCA model applied in previous studies (i.e., IMPACT 2002 + and ReCiPe H) is applicable to Europe; hence con- ducting LCA analysis of China's ISI via above model may lead to in- accurate results ( Li et al., 2016a ;  Chen et al., 2016 ). Moreover, the limited LCA research in China mainly concerns air emissions ( Li et al., 2016b ), and no life cycle water footprint analysis of China's crude steel production has been found. Thus, the present study aims to (1) perform a life cycle WF assess- ment based on ISO 14046 standard and a LCA analysis of China's crude steel production, (2) quantify gray and blue WFs of ISI at the national level including direct and indirect processes, (3) analyze the key direct and indirect in ﬂ uencing factors in all crude steel production stages in China, and (4) provide useful suggestions for reducing the environ- mental burdens of ISI. 2. Methodology 2.1. Scope de ﬁ nition In this study, the functional unit is a ton of steel billet (i.e., crude steel), which is a quanti ﬁ ed reference for inventories and results ( ISO 14040, 2006 ).  Fig. 1  illustrates system boundary, and Supplementary Information S1 provides detailed information. Molten iron smelting, steelmaking, on-site reuse of various byproducts (e.g., crude oil and blast furnace gases), and raw material production were all considered in this study. All these processes involve transportation, waste disposal, direct waste emissions, and land occupation. Infrastructure was ex- cluded for its minimal contribution to the overall impact ( Frischknecht et al., 2005 ) and the lack of detailed data. Direct WF represents water consumption in the operation stage and on-site waste (i.e., wastewater, waste gas, and solid) emissions, whereas indirect WF includes the water consumed and the waste discharged in the supply chain (e.g., raw materials production, land ﬁ ll, and transportation). Additionally, steel billets produced by converter steelmaking technology, which is applied in this study, account for approximately 95% of Chinese crude steel output ( CISA, 2016 ). 2.2. Methodology Gray (i.e., water resources used to assimilate various pollutants), blue (i.e., consumptive use of surface and groundwater), and green (i.e., rainwater insofar as it does not become run-o ﬀ ) WFs are generally in- volved in WF analyses ( Hoekstra et al., 2011 ). However, green WF was excluded in this study because rainwater is generally ignored in in- dustrial activities ( Berger et al., 2012 ). The LCA model used in this study ( Fig. 2 ) is a combination of IMPACTWorld+ model ( Plou ﬀ e et al., 2012 ), IPCC report ( IPCC, 2014 ), and ReCiPe model ( Huijbregts et al., 2017 ). Meanwhile, updated characterization factors of carcinogens, non-carcinogens, and freshwater ecotoxicity was conducted based on USEtox ™ model ( Huijbregts et al., 2010 ) by  Li et al. (2016a)  and  Chen et al. (2016 ). Di ﬀ erent from the quanti ﬁ cation of updated character- ization factors of the aforementioned categories in LCA analysis, the quanti ﬁ cation of characterization factors for WF evaluation considered only the intake routes, exposure pathway, and in ﬂ uence related to water quality ( Ma et al., 2018 ). Detailed calculation process is shown in Supplementary Information S2. Moreover, the characterization factor of the remaining midpoints in gray WF evaluation (i.e., aquatic eu- trophication) was similar to that of the LCA model in this study. Blue WF evaluation was conducted according to the investigations reported by  Berger et al. (2014) . Furthermore, the water press index was used to assess the proportion of WF in China's per capita water resources in di ﬀ erent regions ( NBSC, 2016 ). The uncertainty analysis results can be obtained from Supplementary Information S3. 2.3. Key factors The contributions of processes and substances to the main midpoints are shown in  Fig. 4 . For the common categories (i.e., aquatic eu- trophication, carcinogens, and non-carcinogens) in LCA and WF ana- lyses ( Fig. 4 a), transportation, iron ore mining, coal-based electricity generation, and magnesium oxide production dominated the impact. Ferroalloy production and direct wastewater discharge were re- sponsible for the remaining proportion of aquatic eutrophication. However, compared with the situation in the gray WF evaluation, direct emissions played more e ﬀ ective roles in the LCA analysis. Similar re- sults were obtained for blue WF in which direct freshwater consump- tion contributed 28.8% to the total value. Meanwhile, the contribution of transportation in gray WF analysis was evident ( Fig. 4 a). Aside from high environmental burdens, the economic cost of logistics in China's ISI (10.5% of the total cost) is higher than that at the international advanced level which is 6.6% in Japan ( CISA, 2016 ). In this study, transportation was assumed to involve the use of a lorry for solid waste for 50 km distance and a rail for raw materials for 100 km distance. Regardless of the type of transportation (e.g., rail, road, and water), a linear relationship exists between transport distance and its environ- mental impact ( Hong et al., 2015 ). Therefore, WF can be reduced by selecting a reasonable transportation distance and mode. Among the remaining key categories in the LCA analysis ( Fig. 4 b), coal mining was the most important factor for fossil depletion, followed by iron ore mining and coal-based electricity generation. The three aforementioned processes contributed 29.0% and 37.8% to global warming and re- spiratory inorganics, respectively, in which direct emissions and quick lime production played e ﬀ ective roles (i.e., 63.8% for global warming and 39.1% for respiratory inorganics). Transportation and silico- manganese acquisition dominated the in ﬂ uence in the remaining pro- portion of respiratory inorganics. Additionally,  Olmez et al. (2016)  and Huang et al. (2010)  reported that sintering is the key process of steel production. This result is consistent with the results in  Fig. S5 . COD, phosphate, and BOD 5  were responsible for aquatic eu- trophication, and they were basically from indirect processes (e.g., iron ore mining, external transport, and ferroalloy production). In addition, direct wastewater emissions contributed 8.5% to the e ﬀ ects of COD. Hexavalent chromium [Cr (VI)] to water generated from indirect emissions of magnesium oxide, iron ore mining, and transportation was the dominant substance for carcinogens in the WF analysis. Meanwhile, mercury (Hg) to air constituted 25.0% of the carcinogens score in LCA analysis. For non-carcinogens in WF analysis, Hg emissions, followed by arsenic (As) to air and titanium (Ti) to soil were the most signi ﬁ cant contributing factors. All of these factors were mainly generated through indirect processes, expect for Hg to air, in which 24.8% of its in ﬂ uence originated from direct emissions caused by coal incineration. Moreover, Hg to air dominated the impact in the LCA analysis, given that ap- proximately 94% of the total contribution mainly stemmed from the supply chain. Coal and carbon dioxide played vital roles in fossil de- pletion and global warming, respectively. Nitrogen oxides, sulfur dioxide, and particulates contributed 51.0%, 33.3%, and 14.8% to re- spiratory inorganics, respectively. 3.3. Sensitivity analysis 3.3.1. Sensitivity to main contributors The sensitivity analysis of key factors ( Fig. 5 ) was conducted to analyze the e ﬀ ects of changes in data on the results ( ISO 14044, 2006 ). For gray WF, the e ﬃ ciency of magnesium oxide production, iron ore mining, and transportation obtained high environmental bene ﬁ t. The e ﬃ ciency of ferroalloy, electricity, and direct WF also exhibited a sig- ni ﬁ cant in ﬂ uence. Improvements in direct freshwater consumption and iron ore mining played dominant roles in blue WF reduction, followed by transportation, electricity, and ferroalloy. These  ﬁ ndings indicate the importance of reducing WF in the supply chain (i.e., raw material production and its transportation). A similar analysis of each key midpoint in WF and LCA analyses can be observed from  Fig. 5 . 3.3.2. Sensitivity to direct wastewater discharge in gray WF analysis China's discharge standard for ISI pollutants is mainly concentrated in the atmosphere and involves SO 2 , dust, heavy metals, Benz(a)pyrene, and other pollutants ( MPI, 2016 ), especially in blast furnaces ( MEPC, 1996a ), sintering furnaces ( MEPC, 2012a , b ), and coke ovens ( MEPC, 1996b .) Limited standards for steelmaking wastewater, which focuses on the indicators of COD, pH, suspended solids, NH 4 -N, and oils, are applied currently ( MEPC, 2012a , b ). Accordingly, enterprise self-mon- itoring indicators of water pollutants show low representativeness. To solve this problem, the aggregated data ( Tables S3 - S5 ) was used to assess the gray WF of direct wastewater emissions at national level. The proportion of direct wastewater emissions increased from 1.15% to 5.14%. The lack of enterprise self-monitoring indicators including phosphorus, BOD, Cr (VI), Cu, Zn, Ni, As, and Cd were responsible for this variation. Either the discharge standard of water pollutants or the cleaner production standard ignores the emissions of aforementioned pollutants during crude steel production, except for Zn and As emis- sions during iron making and sintering, respectively ( MEPC, 2012a , b ; MEPC, 2006 ). Thus, abundant controlling indicators of water pollutants should be implemented instead of limited indicators (e.g., COD, pH, and NH 4 -N) to avoid the misleading of WF ( Maes et al., 2009 ). These results indicate that the government should systematically and scienti ﬁ cally publish standards and policies based on the actual situation of in- dustries. 3.4. National average WF of ISI The key factors identi ﬁ ed by aforementioned analysis were replaced by national statistics data to assess the national average WF on the basis of  Hong et al. (2015)  investigation (Supplementary information S6). The national average gray and blue WFs of ISI in China were 1.04 × 10 10   m 3   and 5.84 × 10 9   m 3 , respectively. Gray WF contributed more signi ﬁ cantly than blue WF did. The key processes of gray WF at national level showed di ﬀ erences when compared with those at the enterprise level ( Fig. S7 ). A signi ﬁ cant decrease was observed in iron ore mining (23.4% - 14.7%) because of the import (932.51 million ton) and export (60,000 tons) of iron ores ( NBSC, 2016 ). Correspondingly, a signi ﬁ cant increasing in direct WF (3.4% - 10.9%) and electricity (5.7% - 8.3%) and was observed. Missing inventories of wastewater ef- ﬂ uents and relatively low electricity consumption and in the reported steel production site were the main reasons. The national direct was- tewater discharge of China's ISI was around 717 million tons ( CISA, 2016 ), which accounted for approximately 6% of gray WF caused by national industrial wastewater discharge ( NBSC, 2016 ). However, the gray WF caused by freshwater ecotoxicity was 22.8 times that of the national value. Unreliable national statistics, which are often pressur- ized by local government ( Guan et al., 2012 ;  Liu and Yang, 2009 ), re- sulted in the inaccurate evaluation in freshwater ecotoxicity. Conclusions This study performed LCA and WF analyses of ISI in China and explored the key factors that contribute to environmental burdens. The LCA analysis showed that the environmental degeneration caused by crude steel production was mainly derived from metal depletion, fossil depletion, respiratory inorganics, global warming, freshwater ecotoxi- city, and non-carcinogens. In this study, 1 ton of crude steel production obtained 12.60 m 3   of gray WF at the national level in China, and the score was 1.8 times that of blue WF (7.09 m 3 ). Compared with the re- sults of the LCA analysis, the WF midpoints had lower scores in carci- nogens, non-carcinogens, and freshwater ecotoxicity categories. This result indicates that the life cycle impact assessment model cannot be directly applied in WF analysis because LCA involves intake routes and exposure pathways unrelated to water quality ( Fig. S3 ). The most im- portant contributor to gray WF was aquatic eutrophication, whose ef- fects were dominated by indirect processes (95.7%), such as iron ore mining, raw material transportation, and electricity generation, which were also the key factors in LCA analysis. Direct emissions also played important roles in global warming and respiratory inorganics. Im- provement of these processes would reduce the environmental burden. COD, Cr (VI) to water, phosphate to water, BOD 5 , Hg to air and soil, and As to air were the key substances for gray WF. Meanwhile, Hg emissions provided a large contribution in LCA analysis (e.g., 25.0% for carci- nogens and 96.5% for non-carcinogens). Moreover, nitrogen oxides, particulates, and sulfur dioxide were the main substances for re- spiratory inorganics, whereas carbon dioxide dominantly in ﬂ uenced global warming. Additionally, water stress caused by direct wastewater discharge varied at the provincial level, indicating that the WF analysis model should consider regional variations in the quantity and quality of water resources. Furthermore, numerous indicators, including COD, SS, NH 4 -N, oils, phosphorus, BOD, Cr (VI), Cu, Zn, Ni, As, and Cd, should be implemented for controlling pollutant emissions. This study provides valuable information for water resource man- agement, enterprise layout, and scienti ﬁ c pollution control of ISI. Moreover, this study is meaningful for understanding the di ﬀ erences between WF and LCA analyses, and it can provide some useful ideas about how the WF is appropriately conducted via LCA model. However, some limitations still existed in this study. Although converter tech- nology, which is widely used in China, was analyzed in this study, other technologies should also be investigated further. Moreover, studies on dynamic database and spatial disparity in national WF evaluation and sustainable WF assessments, including economic and social, should be involved. Our research group will address these limitations in our fu- ture work.
